# Planning per round, minds by tier, and what 3v3 costs

This follows the perf study (`../perf/RESULTS.md`). It asks:

1. whether planning once per round, with light reactive decisions inside the round, keeps the
   quality of re-planning every exchange;
2. whether plans can be reused between exchanges and rounds;
3. whether one planner, with parameters scaled by intellect and art expertise, gives a ladder of
   minds from dull to sharp;
4. what it costs when every fighter plans, especially at 3v3;
5. what else avoids recalculating.

It is a throwaway measurement. **Nothing under `src/` was changed.** Every number comes from runs
recorded in `out/*.json`; `out/tables.md` has every table, generated by `report.test.ts`.

## Answers in brief

- **Plan once per round: yes, with one surprise.** The round plan is almost always one card,
  then the fighter's own default plan. In bench 1v1s, re-planning every exchange won 42/72
  fights, planning per round 37/72, and today's engine AI 26/72. Per round is 2.5× cheaper in
  simulated exchanges (222 against 562 a round). The cheapest good "reactive layer" turned out
  to be the **default plan the round plan was scored with**. It is a conditional priority list
  the engine already evaluates live. On top of it, three cheap checks re-plan when needed: the
  card can't be played, a fact it relied on didn't come true, or a call trigger fired. They cost
  under 12 µs a fighter an exchange, and fire about 0.4 times a round.
- **Plan reuse: within the round, yes; between rounds and by cache, no.** Re-scoring only the
  plan's tail and its top four alternatives each exchange (a "warm" decision, no fresh candidate
  set) matched re-planning every exchange in bench 1v1s (43/72 against 42/72) at 56% of the
  simulated exchanges. Its choice agreed with a fresh plan 81% of the time, about the noise
  floor of the fresh plan itself. But on fewer seeds it does worse than keeping the default
  continuation (see 3). Warm starts between rounds saved little: fights are short, so many round
  starts are a fight's first, and a trigger forces a full plan. Skipping a re-plan when the
  situation is unchanged hit under 1% of decisions.
- **One planner, scaled: yes.** The same code runs from E to S. Only the candidate count, chain
  length, seeds, belief blur, telegraph reading and between-step policy change.
  - Against a C mind (same build, same stats, 80 fights each): net health per fight goes
    E −15, D −11, C 0, B +3, A +3, S +8. Wins of 80: 21, 27, 31, 33, 31, 34. S beats E 36 to 24
    over 80 fights.
  - The top tier wins more but is far from unbeatable. Fight noise is larger than the tier
    effect: a mirror between bench builds is mostly decided by the dice.
  - The dull tier is coherent. As the player's side against today's AI it wins 24/60, against
    the AI's own 25/60. As a foe it is a little worse than the AI (17/60 against 24/60).
  - Emergent combos appear by tier. Hide → killing blow appears at **D** (E never sees the Veil:
    it isn't among its three first cards). Daze → finisher is played from E, rising to its most
    at B and A. The assassin's Paper Veil → Pinched Flame is rare at every tier.
- **3v3 cost is the problem, and it is per-exchange cost, not decisions.**
  - One simulated exchange costs 1.5–2.1 ms at 3v3, against about 0.5 ms at 1v1.
  - With every fighter planning at its own intellect grade (the chūnin cast is D and C), a 3v3
    round costs 440–800 ms (p95 650–1,060 ms) on this machine. Of that, 320–580 ms is at the
    round's start and at most about 160 ms in any one gap between exchanges.
  - Everyone at A costs 1.9–2.7 s a round; everyone at S costs 8 s.
  - Re-planning every exchange with the perf study's GOAP r8 costs 4.5–5.3 s a round (p95
    8.5 s).
  - On a phone 3–5× slower, the own-grade mix is 1.3–4 s a round. That fits behind the watch
    screen's replay and the plan screen only if it runs off the main thread and the budget is
    capped by fighter count. Sharp mixes don't fit without that cap.
- **What else saves work:**
  - not deciding between steps at all for dull and middle minds: their gap cost is 0 at the
    median;
  - not planning a fighter who can't act;
  - not planning the player's own fighter (5 planners in a 3v3, not 6);
  - a fight-wide budget split by tier.

  Shared rollouts across allies and situation caching saved nothing measurable.

## Method

- **Machine.** One core of a 4-core Intel Xeon @ 2.10 GHz (cloud VM), Node 22, single-threaded.
  - Another session's benches shared the machine during most runs, with load average 2–7. All ms
    from those runs are marked contended; compare their rows with each other.
  - The key timings (`timing.test.ts`, `q4.test.ts`) were re-run with nothing else of mine
    running, at load average about 1.0–1.2. Those are the ms quoted in the answers.
  - "Simulated exchanges" (real `resolveExchange` calls inside planning) is the machine-free
    measure of cost. Rows report it beside ms.
- **The forward model** (`model.ts`) is `../perf/sim.ts` made side-generic.
  - A player's-side fighter is steered by forcing its loadout. A foe is steered by forcing its
    intent, the card it telegraphs, which is what a foe's plan already is in today's engine.
  - Prefix states are kept per seed (exact, as the perf study proved).
- **Silent exchanges.** Narration is silenced with the perf study's mocks
  (`../perf/silent/setup.ts`): told lines become the non-empty constant `'x'`, which the perf
  study checked changes no outcome in 2,648 of 2,648 rollouts. The log is emptied before each
  exchange.
  - **I did not apply the `if (lost !== null)` fix** from `combat/narration-determinism`. The
    constants are non-empty, so the empty-line bug can't fire.
- **The planner** (`mind.ts`) is ONE function, `plan()`. Every tier runs it.
  - Candidates:
    - the engine's own pick now;
    - the top `firstK` first cards by a prior that costs no exchange (the engine's pick, then
      attacks by `cardWorth`, then the rest in kit order);
    - GOAP enabling chains (`../perf/goap.ts`, preconditions read from verbs) of length 2
      (`chain` 1) or 3 (`chain` 2);
    - optionally the best first card extended by every card (`widthOne`).
  - Each sequence is scored over `rollouts` seeds to `horizon` exchanges, with the fighter's own
    default plan after it, on the fight as the mind believes it:
    - `blur`: every attribute of every other fighter is off by a fixed ±blur for the fight;
    - `telegraph`: whether it reads the foes' announced next card. Only the player's side gets
      telegraphs.
  - **Expertise:** a chain whose cards are all in one art gets the chain length of the better of
    the mind's grade and that art's grade. A taijutsu master sees taijutsu pairs with a dull mind.
- **Closed loop** (`loop.ts`). Foes decide first, so their decision is their telegraph. Then the
  player's side decides, seeing it. The exchange is resolved on the fight's own seeds, which no
  planner sees.
  - Policies:
    - `exchange`: re-plan before every exchange;
    - `round`: plan at the round's start, then keep the plan unless the reactive checks
      invalidate it;
    - `blind`: never re-check;
    - `default`: today's engine AI;
    - `warm`: within a round, re-score the tail and the top alternatives only.
  - Reactive checks:
    - the next card is unplayable (`fromSlot`, or for a foe `playable`);
    - a fact the step relied on doesn't hold: `open`, `unseen`, `at:<band>`;
    - a trigger fired that hadn't this fight: the player's calls (summoned, held, hurt <1/3,
      killer, ally down, revealed), generalised to any fighter.
- **Fights.**
  - The two prototype scenarios: 30 fights each, capped at 8 exchanges, as the perf study ran
    them.
  - The perf study's 12 bench pairs (`PAIRS`, chūnin cast, the first planning against today's AI
    foe): 6 seeds each, capped at 16 exchanges (4 rounds).
  - Mirror matches of bench builds for the tiers.
  - Teams from `matrixCast` for 2v2 and 3v3.
  - All seeds are fixed. Same code and seed give the same fight: two separate runs of
    "round + reactive" gave identical rows.
- **Audit (regret).** Where a step was played without a fresh full plan (a kept step, the
  default continuation, a light or warm decision):
  - the same mind plans afresh from the same state (untimed);
  - a referee scores both sequences on the true state over 16 seeds no planner uses.
  - Regret is the referee's value of the fresh choice minus the played one's, in health points
    over the horizon. It can be negative.
  - **The fresh plan is itself noisy at 8 seeds.** The perf study's reference agreed with
    itself 76% of the time. Agreement near 80% is about as good as it can show.
- **Sample sizes.**
  - 30–80 fights a cell. The standard deviation of a win count is about 4 at 60–80 fights, so
    differences under about 6 wins are noise.
  - The bench pairs and the 3v3 cost runs are small (72 fights, and 8 fights a mix).

## Q1. Plan once per round, with light reactive decisions

All policies use the same search, the perf study's GOAP r8 (every first card, enabling pairs,
8 seeds, horizon 2). Only the policy differs. The pair rows are 72 fights (12 pairs × 6). ms are
contended. Simulated exchanges are the cost.

| Policy                                                    | daze → finisher won /30 | hide → blade won /30 | bench pairs won /72 | pairs dealt / taken | sim exchanges a round (pairs) | plans a round (pairs) | re-plans for invalidation a round | audit: agree, regret |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------: | -------------------: | ------------------: | ------------------: | ----------------------------: | --------------------: | --------------------------------: | -------------------- |
| today's engine AI                                         |                       2 |                   11 |                  26 |             74 / 80 |                             0 |                     0 |                                 — | —                    |
| re-plan every exchange                                    |                  **15** |               **30** |              **42** |         76 / **65** |                           562 |                  3.50 |                                 — | —                    |
| **round + reactive (re-plan when invalidated)**           |                  **15** |                   29 |                  37 |             78 / 74 |                       **222** |                  1.40 |                              0.39 | 388: 39%, 5.9        |
| round + reactive (fall back to default when invalid)      |                      11 |                   29 |                  31 |             74 / 77 |                           161 |                  1.00 |                              0.33 | —                    |

| **every exchange, warm within the round** (Q2)            |                      11 |                   29 |              **43** |             76 / 68 |                       **317** |                  3.38 |                                 — | **431: 81%, 1.6**    |

Light decisions are the same planner with a tiny budget: singletons only, four first cards, two
seeds (`lightOf`). The audit column counts steps played without a fresh plan, then gives
agreement with a fresh plan and mean regret.

What it shows:

- **The round plan is almost always one card.**
  - A pair ties with "the card, then the default plan", because the default plan plays the
    pay-off as soon as its condition holds. Per round, 0.05 planned steps were kept and about 2
    exchanges were left to the default plan.
  - So "plan once per round" in practice means: choose the round's opening card by search, and
    let the default plan's live conditions play the rest. The default plan is exactly the policy
    the search assumed when it scored that card.
  - Q3 shows the same for every tier that keeps its plan: 1.3–1.7 plans a round, re-plans
    included.
- **The reactive checks pay.** Blind and fall-back-to-default win 31/72; re-planning on
  invalidation wins 37/72.
  - Invalidations a round: triggers 0.24, unplayable 0.08, facts missing 0.07. Each re-plan is a
    full plan.
  - The checks cost 7–12 µs a fighter an exchange (`timing.test.ts`). They are negligible.
- **Per round loses a little against every exchange:** 37 against 42 of 72, and 9 more health
  taken a fight, for 2.5× fewer simulated exchanges. The audit says why. Where the default plan
  played, a fresh plan agreed only 39% of the time, at a regret of 5.9 a step. The default plan
  is a decent continuation but not the planner's best.
- **Light decisions are not the answer.** A two-seed singleton search between steps chose by
  noise. In daze → finisher it stepped back out of reach every time and won 1/30, where the
  default plan's continuation won 15. In the pairs it was no better than keeping the plan. A
  light search must have enough seeds to beat the default plan, and at that point it is a warm
  re-score.
- **The warm re-score is the best per-exchange option** (Q2): 43/72 at 317 simulated exchanges a
  round. That is the quality of re-planning every exchange at 56% of the cost.
- **Longer horizons hurt.** Horizon 4 (score the whole round) chose kiting in daze → finisher
  (9/30) and costs 2×. The perf study saw the same at 16 seeds. Kiting scores well on the
  objective over 4 exchanges and loses fights on the cap.
- **The surprise check (re-plan when health is more than 15 off the plan's expectation) fires
  0.6 times a round.** It cost 50% more and won 39/72, within noise of plain round + reactive.
  Not worth a rule.

## Q2. Plan reuse and caching

Same search (GOAP r8). Warm means re-scoring only the plan's unplayed tail and the top four
sequences of the last plan, whole and without their first card, plus the engine's pick, with
no fresh candidate set. ms are contended.

| Policy                                                                      | daze /30 | hide /30 | pairs /72 | sim exchanges a round (pairs) | audit: agree, regret (pairs)  |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------: | -------: | --------: | ----------------------------: | ----------------------------- |
| re-plan every exchange (Q1)                                                 |       15 |       30 |        42 |                           562 | —                             |
| every exchange, warm within the round (full at round start and on triggers) |       11 |       29 |    **43** |                       **317** | 431: **81%, 1.6**             |
| every exchange, skip when the situation is unchanged                        |       15 |       30 |        40 |                           560 | —                             |
| round + reactive (Q1)                                                       |       15 |       29 |        37 |                           222 | 388: 39%, 5.9 (default steps) |
| round, warm between rounds (full every 2nd round)                           |       15 |       29 |        35 |                           183 | 77: 70%, 3.8                  |
| round, warm between rounds (full only on a trigger)                         |       15 |       29 |        35 |                           172 | 98: 72%, 3.9                  |
| round + reactive, skip when unchanged                                       |       15 |       29 |        35 |                           222 | —                             |

- **Warm within the round works.**
  - A warm decision simulates 49–126 exchanges, against 104–514 for a full plan of the same
    tier. Alone, it takes 22 ms against 43 ms at B, and 62 ms against 248 ms at S.
  - It keeps quality where the full plan has enough seeds: 81% agreement with a fresh plan, at
    regret 1.6.
  - At `hide → blade` its regret is high (26 on 47 audits), because a few states rank Veil
    against Blade differently. It still won 29/30.
- **Warm between rounds saves little, because fights are short.**
  - Of 285 round starts (both scenarios and the pairs), 132 were a fight's first round, with
    nothing to reuse.
  - Another 33 followed a trigger (hurt, held, killer…), which demands a fresh plan.
  - It saved 18–23% of simulated exchanges, and lost 2 wins in 72, which is noise.
- **Caching by situation is useless here.** A coarse key (each fighter's health and chakra in
  tenths, conditions, band and the telegraphs) repeated on 8 of about 1,000 decisions. The perf
  study found the same of its leaf memo: health moves every exchange.
- **What a warm re-score can't do** is notice a new idea: a card that wasn't in the last plan's
  top four. So it needs a full plan on every trigger and every round start. That is what the
  measured policy does.

What it shows:

- **One planner gives a ladder.**
  - In net health against a C mind it is monotone except A ≈ B, from −15 to +8.
  - In wins it rises from 21 to 34 of 80.
  - The tier effect is real but small next to fight noise. The bench mirror matches are close
    by design, and the dice decide most of them.
  - The top tier is not unbeatable. S beat E only 36 to 24.
  - Against today's AI every tier from C up does a little better than the AI itself, as both
    player and foe.
- **The dull end is coherent, not crippled.**
  - E plays the same search on three first cards, two seeds, a ±30% wrong read of the foe and no
    telegraph. It is never a separate script.
  - As the player's side against the engine AI it ties the AI (24 against 25 of 60).
  - As a foe it is somewhat worse than today's AI (17 against 24 of 60, −7 net health). That is
    within about two standard deviations, so partly noise, but the direction is consistent.
  - If dull foes must not fall below today's AI, give E four first cards. The cost is one more
    candidate.
- **Where combos appear is set by `firstK`, not by chain depth.** In round mode the default
  continuation plays the pay-off once its condition holds. So "the enabler, then default" finds
  the combo without any chain, if the enabler is among the first cards considered.
  - E's prior ranks attacks first, so its three never include the Veil: it throws the blade
    unhidden.
  - D's four do include it, so hide → blade appears at D, from intellect alone.
  - Daze → finisher appears from E and peaks at B and A.
  - Chains matter in states where the default plan wouldn't play the pay-off, and that is where
    C and up gain.
- **Expertise.** At chūnin scale the cast's arts are C. So expertise lifts E and D minds to
  pairs within their art, and it changed little in the tier runs. In the scenarios
  (`combos-x1-v2`) it added nothing over intellect for E and D.
  - The prototype fighters are B in every art, so with expertise on, E and D get pairs in an
    art. Their results didn't change (daze 5/20 and hide 7/20 for E either way). E's miss is
    the Veil not being among its first cards, which expertise doesn't touch.
  - Expertise as designed is cheap: it only raises chain length for single-art chains.
- **Re-scoring between steps is only worth it on enough seeds.**
  - In v2, B (6 seeds) re-scored every exchange. It did worse than C (22 against 42 wins against
    C) and worse than v1's B, which kept the default continuation (+6.5 net).
  - In v3, B keeps the plan: 33 against 32.
  - Each re-decision on few seeds is another chance to pick noise.
  - So a mind should re-decide between steps only when its seeds make it better than the default
    plan's live conditions. Here that is about 12–16 seeds or more.
- **Longer horizons and wider search are not "sharper".**
  - v1's S (horizon 3, triples, 12 seeds) cost 1.2–1.6 s a round in a 1v1 and kited in
    daze → finisher.
  - Every sharp setting tends to find stepping out of reach. It scores well on dealt minus
    taken, and it loses wins on the cap.
  - Triples (chain 2) found nothing the pairs plus default continuation didn't.

| 3v3     | GOAP r8, round + reactive |             2,292–2,666 |   3,044–4,160 |           1,399–1,426 |          1,741–1,794 |            142–243 / 639–1,083 |
| 3v3     | GOAP r8, every exchange   |             4,455–5,281 |   8,419–8,485 |           2,824–2,862 |          1,441–1,801 |      1,042–1,372 / 2,266–2,339 |

(Ranges are line-up A to B. The earlier contended run, `q4-alone.json` at load 2–4, gives
figures within about 10% for the same mixes under ladder v2.)

- **Per-exchange cost grows with fighters.** A 3v3 exchange costs 1.5–2.1 ms here, against
  about 0.5 ms at 1v1, and the number of planners grows too. So 3v3 is about 7–10× a 1v1 for the
  same minds, not 3×.
- **Per round against per exchange.**
  - At the same search (GOAP r8), per round with checks costs 2.3–2.7 s a round at 3v3.
    Re-planning every exchange costs 4.5–5.3 s.
  - With the ladder at the cast's own grades, a 3v3 round is 0.44–0.80 s, which is 6–10× less.
  - Most of it falls at the round's start (321–580 ms). Inside the round, a gap costs 0 at the
    median and about 150 ms at p95: someone's plan was invalidated and re-planned.
- **On a phone.**
  - Assume a mid-range phone runs this JavaScript 3–5× slower than one core of this 2.1 GHz
    Xeon. That is an assumption, not measured: a mid-range Android's big core is roughly that
    against a server core, and a WebView's JIT is no better than Node's.
  - Then the own-grade 3v3 round is about 1.3–4.0 s, with 1.0–2.9 s at the round's start and
    0.4–0.8 s in a worst gap.
  - All-A teams would be 6–13 s a round, and all-S 25–40 s. Those can't run as they are.
- **Can it run while the player plans?** Mostly, if it runs in a Web Worker and is budgeted.
  - The foes' round plans must be ready when the plan screen opens, because their first card is
    the telegraph. But they depend only on the state after the round's last exchange. That state
    is known the moment the exchange resolves, and the watch screen then replays it for a few
    seconds.
  - Allies' plans can also use the plan screen's time (seconds) before the player commits.
  - A gap's re-plans depend only on the exchange just resolved, so they can be computed during
    that exchange's replay.
  - None of it needs to block a frame if it runs off the main thread.
  - The risk is the fast-forward or skip button: a player who skips replays makes the budget
    visible as a wait.

## Q5. What else avoids recalculating

| Idea                                                                                                    | Measured                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Keep the plan inside the round (dull and middle minds)                                                  | E–A: mid-round gaps cost 0 at the median at every size (Q4). Re-plans only on invalidation, about 0.4 a round.                                                                                      |
| Don't plan a fighter who can't act                                                                      | Built in (a dazed fighter loses its turn and is not planned for). Not counted separately.                                                                                                           |
| Don't plan the player's own fighter                                                                     | The player plans it. That makes 5 planners in a 3v3, not 6: about −15%.                                                                                                                             |
| Skip when the situation is unchanged                                                                    | Under 1% of decisions (Q2). Not worth it.                                                                                                                                                           |
| Warm re-score instead of a fresh plan                                                                   | 2–4× cheaper a decision (Q3 cost table). Worth it only for minds with enough seeds (Q3).                                                                                                            |
| Warm start between rounds                                                                               | 18–23% fewer simulated exchanges, but most round starts follow a trigger (Q2). Small.                                                                                                               |
| Shared rollouts across allies                                                                           | Nothing to share as built. ## Recommendations

1. **Plan once per round, and keep the plan, for all but the sharpest minds.** At the round's
   start, run the tier's search. Play its first card (for a foe, that is its telegraph). Then
   let the default plan's live conditions play the round, exactly as the search assumed.
   Re-plan only when:
   - the next card can't be played;
   - a fact the plan relied on (`open`, `unseen`, a band) didn't come true;
   - a call trigger fires for that fighter (summoned, held, hurt, killer, ally down, revealed),
     each once a fight. That mirrors the player's calls (design Part 8, "breaks mirror calls").

   This is 2.5× cheaper than re-planning every exchange. It loses about 5 wins in 72 in bench
   1v1s. It still opens with the emergent combos.

2. **Give only the sharpest minds a warm re-score each exchange.** Re-score the plan's tail and
   its top four alternatives, with no fresh candidates, on at least about 12–16 seeds. On fewer
   seeds it is worse than keeping the plan. Never use a cheap light search between steps: on two
   seeds it chose by noise and threw away a combo.
3. **One planner, this ladder** (v3 above). Adjust E to four first cards if dull foes must not
   fall below today's AI.
   - Search parameters by intellect grade: first cards, chains, seeds, belief blur, telegraph
     reading, between steps.
   - Chain length by the better of intellect and the chain's art grade (expertise).
   - Horizon 2 at every tier: a longer one buys kiting, not quality.
4. **A cost budget a round, enforced fight-wide.** Measured here; phone at 3–5×.

   | Fight | Budget a round (sim exchanges, all planners) | ≈ ms here     | ≈ phone     | In one exchange gap                  |
   | ----- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------ |
   | 1v1   | 900 (fits two S minds' own search)           | ≈ 450         | 1.4–2.3 s   | ≤ 150 exchanges (≈ 75 ms here)       |
   | 2v2   | 600                                          | ≈ 600         | 1.8–3 s     | ≤ 100 (≈ 100 ms)                     |
   | 3v3   | **400**                                      | **≈ 600–800** | **1.8–4 s** | **≤ 80 (≈ 150 ms; 0.5–0.8 s phone)** |
   - Split the budget between planners by tier weight. Use E 1, D 2, C 4, B 6, A 9 and S 36,
     from the 1v1 cost a round (22, 40, 85, 134, 203 and 800 simulated exchanges). When the
     split is short, scale each planner's seeds and first cards down together.
   - The chūnin cast's own grades fit the 3v3 budget as they are (294–384 a round).
   - All-A or all-S teams must be scaled down by fighter count. One S foe among five D minds
     in a 3v3 would get about 290 a round, roughly an A mind's 1v1 search.
   - Run planning in a Web Worker:
     - foes' round plans during the last exchange's replay;
     - allies' plans during the plan screen;
     - gap re-plans during the replay of the exchange before.

5. **Keep the real-exchange planner as the referee, and keep looking for a cheaper forward
   model for 3v3.** Exchange cost per fighter is what makes 3v3 expensive. A reduced-fight model
   or joint rollouts are the next things worth measuring, against this planner as the reference.

## Surprises and what didn't work

- **The round plan is one card.** Pairs tie with "card, then the default plan". So the default
  plan, today's engine AI, is the reactive layer, and planning per round is mostly choosing each
  round's opening move.
- **The default plan is a better between-step policy than a cheap search.** Light searches at 2
  seeds lost a combo scenario outright (1/30). Warm re-scores at 6 seeds made B weaker than C.
- **Sharper is not always better.** S with horizon 3 or 16 seeds tends to kite. That scores well
  on the objective and wins fewer capped fights. It also costs 10–30× a C mind.
- **Expertise barely mattered in these runs.** At chūnin scale every cast member's best art is
  C, and pairs mattered less than which first cards a mind looks at.
- **The tier effect is small.** Even S against E is 36 to 24 over 80 fights. Bench mirrors are
  dice-heavy. A ladder you can feel may need the identity layer (Part 4) and beliefs (Part 5),
  not just search budget.
- **Not done:**
  - expertise-off tier runs against the AI and against C (only the scenarios were run both
    ways);
  - jōnin-scale casts, where art and intellect grades differ more;
  - any 3v3 outcome comparison (only cost);
  - the fight-wide budget, reduced-fight model and joint rollouts.
