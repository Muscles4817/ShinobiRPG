Faces that belong to someone

Today a portrait is one face with a hairstyle and colours changed, so everyone reads as the same person. Here every face is put together from parts: face shape, neck and build, eye shape, brows, nose, ears and expression, with age lines, facial hair and marks on top. Twelve parts with five to seven choices each give hundreds of thousands of faces before any colour changes.

A cast

Twelve people from the same kit. Cover the hair and they still differ: Tetsu's round face and wide-set narrow eyes, Genkurō's long jaw and hooked nose, Ayame's angular cheekbones and upturned eyes.

One person, many moods

An expression moves the mouth together with the brows, the lower lids and the cheeks, so a smile shows in the eyes too. The game could pick the mood from the scene: a grin after a win, worry on the road, a shout mid-fight.

At avatar size

The hub's "Who's here" row shows people at about 40px. Face shape, skin, brows and expression still tell them apart there.

How it would reach the game

New appearance fields. Appearance gains face, build, eyes, brows, nose, ears, mouth, age, facialHair and marks, each a string-literal union like hairStyle.

Nobody is left with the default face. Where a person's data leaves a part out, it is read from a hash of their id. Every authored person and every generated genin gets a distinct, stable face without editing content, and an old save keeps the face it always had. Authored characters can still pin any part.

Creation gets the parts as choices. The character file adds face, eyes, nose, brows and ears pickers beside hair and colours.

Mood follows the moment. A view model names an expression for each place a face appears (a bond scene, a debrief, a fight token), and the portrait draws it.
