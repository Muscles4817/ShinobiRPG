# The Witcher 3 quests: research notes

## Scope and method

These are notes taken to gather raw material, not a design. They cover about forty quests from The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt (2015) and its expansions Hearts of Stone (2015) and Blood and Wine (2016): main quests, secondary quests, witcher contracts, small quests and the main line of each expansion. After them comes a section on what CD Projekt Red's developers and some critics have said about how the studio builds quests. For each quest the notes say who gives it and what they ask, how it plays out (investigation, the turn, the choices), what follows (straight away and later), any notable device, and the sources.

**How this was gathered.** A first pass was written from web-search summaries only, because every direct page fetch was blocked. A second pass checked it against real pages:

- **Opened and read in the second pass:** the witcher.fandom.com page of every quest below, as wikitext through the wiki's MediaWiki API (the normal page URLs return 403), plus a few character and item pages (Anna Strenger, The Ghost in the Tree, Ealdorman of Downwarren, Hubert Rejk, Gaunter O'Dimm, Birna, Orianna, Dettlaff, Letter to Arnvald, the Witcher 3 decision checklist). On Wikipedia: "The Bloody Baron" (the Family Matters article), "The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt", and the two expansion articles.
- **Not opened:** the press and guide sites (PC Gamer, Kotaku, GameSpot, Game Informer, GamesRadar, Notebookcheck, Game Developer, GDC Vault, Gigazine, Filmstories, Fextralife, Gamebanshee and the rest) were refused by the egress proxy in this pass too. So the developer quotes in the last section that Wikipedia doesn't carry still rest on search summaries, and are marked as such.
- Quest facts below now follow the fandom pages. Where the first pass was wrong, the entry has been corrected in place rather than annotated. Where a detail is still only from a summary or from memory, it is marked _(unverified)_. The fandom wiki is fan-written; where it contradicts itself, the entry says so.

## Main quests

### Family Matters (the Bloody Baron)

- **Where and kind:** Act I, Velen (Crow's Perch). Main quest, several hours long. Designed by Paweł Sasko, written by Karolina Stachyra.
- **Premise:** Phillip Strenger, the "Bloody Baron", will tell Geralt what he knows about Ciri only once Geralt finds his wife Anna and daughter Tamara, who went missing in the night. He doesn't call it a flight; he says he was drunk that night and knows nothing of a fight.
- **How it unfolds:** Witcher senses in the bedrooms find signs of a struggle, spilled wine and a hidden talisman. The Baron sends Geralt to the Pellar, who first wants his goat found (the small quest "A Princess in Distress"). The Pellar's augury says Anna was pregnant and miscarried because of the Baron's abuse, and the buried foetus has become a botchling. Back at the keep (a burning stable on the way, with an optional rescue), the drunk Baron picks a fistfight, then admits the beatings, that the women fled, and where he buried the child. **Choice (timed, at the grave):** kill the botchling, or have the Baron carry it home for an elven naming ritual (he names it Dea) so it becomes a lubberkin, a household guardian. On the carry, wraiths attack and Geralt must calm the botchling with Axii after each wave; if he is slow, it turns and the quest falls to the kill route. The lubberkin leads to a smokehouse, Anna's torn-apart horse and the fisherman Voytek's hut. The kill route takes the botchling's blood to the Pellar for a ritual at Wolven Glade (light three braziers, keep relighting them against wraiths). Both routes meet at the fisherman's hut: the women fled with his help, and on the way a fiend carried Anna off, after marks on her hands burned. Tamara reached Oxenfurt; found there (optional), she won't come home and has joined the witch hunters to look for her mother.
- **Consequences:** The botchling choice changes how the quest plays, and the wiki says it has "minimal impact" on the Baron's story. The Baron's ending is decided by The Whispering Hillock and plays out in Return to Crookback Bog (see below). That Anna had made a pact with the Crones to end the pregnancy, in return for a year's service, is revealed only later, in Ladies of the Wood.
- **Devices:** The client is also the villain, and stays sympathetic. The "missing" family turns out to have fled from him, which the clues and his own confession reveal; the abduction that follows (the fiend) is real, but it happened during the flight. Wikipedia: Sasko and Stachyra built the Baron as a contrast to Geralt (both men looking for family, both with a bloody past); gave him charm and humour on purpose so the player feels torn; and timed the reveal of the abuse to come after the player learns he sheltered Ciri. The quest began as one sentence ("Geralt meets a 'Baron' who wants the witcher to kill a monster, and offers information about Ciri in return") and was meant to embody Velen. It won the 2015 Golden Joystick for Best Gaming Moment. _(Unverified, from summaries: a German interview says an earlier, more violent Baron was toned down to keep him sympathetic.)_
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Family_Matters ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_Strenger ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bloody_Baron . Not opened: https://mein-mmo.de/witcher-3-blutiger-baron-hintergrund-interview-ausschnitt/

### Hunting a Witch

- **Where and kind:** Act I, Velen (Midcopse). Main quest, suggested level 5. It starts after "The Nilfgaardian Connection" and can be done before Family Matters.
- **Premise:** Hendrik's notes say Ciri quarrelled with a witch near Midcopse.
- **How it unfolds:** Three ways to find the witch, all usable: eavesdrop on two gossiping women (they hush if Geralt comes too close), ask an old man with a pipe, or go through a woman whose husband visited the witch for his bad back; he talks for 50 crowns or Axii, or if left alone he walks to the witch and can be saved from nekkers on the way. The trail runs past a pond and a tall boulder to a cart guarded by nekkers, then to a hut where villagers are asking the "witch" for help; she sends them off and goes inside. The hut is empty; a skull by a chalk pentagram opens a portal to a hidden cavern, where the witch is the sorceress Keira Metz. She never met Ciri, but a masked elven mage asked her about Ciri, and she offers to take Geralt to his hideout in elven ruins.
- **Devices:** Several social routes to one fact, one of them depending on staying out of earshot. The "witch" of the rumour is a sorceress in hiding, and not the witch the notes meant (that turns out to be the Crones).
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Hunting_a_Witch

### Wandering in the Dark

- **Where and kind:** Act I, Velen. Main quest, suggested level 6.
- **Premise:** Keira takes Geralt into the elven ruins where the mage hid.
- **How it unfolds:** Just inside, they see the Wild Hunt is already there; Keira's portal goes wrong and splits them up. Morphotic projections the mage left for Ciri, and painted swallows (Zireael), guide the way, with a puzzle keyed to Ciri's horse Kelpie. After a golem and the Hunt's navigator opening portals to the White Frost (Geralt guards Keira under her shield), the boss is Nithral, the first Hunt warrior Geralt fights. A last projection warns Ciri away from the witches of Crookback Bog, which hands the trail on. Keira gives Geralt the Eye of Nehaleni to dispel an illusory wall.
- **Consequences:** It leads to Ladies of the Wood and to Keira's own quest, "Magic Lamp". The Eye of Nehaleni is used again later (in the Kaer Morhen quest "Greenhouse Effect", and in Blood and Wine's "La Cage au Fou").
- **Devices:** Recorded visions that hand on the next lead. An item earned in one quest solves others.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Wandering_in_the_Dark ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Greenhouse_Effect

### Ladies of the Wood

- **Where and kind:** Act I, Velen (Crookback Bog). Main quest, suggested level 6.
- **Premise:** Find the Crones and learn what they know of Ciri.
- **How it unfolds:** Geralt follows a trail of treats to the orphans' huts, run by "Gran", who won't talk to strangers. The children help if he plays hide-and-seek (found with witcher senses) or hands out treats; they draw Gran away and the boy Travik says where the godling Johnny's burrow is. Johnny has lost his voice, which is sealed in a bottle in a harpies' nest on a cliff. Given it back, he says Ciri ran towards the orphanage and talks Gran round. The Crones then speak to Geralt (through Gran and the tapestry): they will tell of Ciri if he first helps the ealdorman of Downwarren with a problem, and give him a dagger so the ealdorman knows who sent him. That problem is The Whispering Hillock. When it is settled, the ealdorman takes back the dagger and cuts off his own left ear as the Crones' payment, and Geralt lays it on a rock at the orphanage. When the Crones make a mark on Gran's hand burn, Geralt recognises Anna Strenger; the Crones say she traded a year's servitude for ending her pregnancy, and that she fattens the orphans for them to eat. Then they tell of Ciri.
- **Consequences:** The Hillock choice made inside this quest decides the fates of Downwarren, the orphans and the Baron's family (see Return to Crookback Bog).
- **Devices:** A chain of favours (orphans, then godling, then Crones, then the ealdorman). The Crones speak through a puppet. Their price, an ear, is paid by someone else and shown only after the deal. The missing wife of another quest is found here, unrecognised until a detail gives her away.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Ladies_of_the_Wood ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Ealdorman_(Downwarren) ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_Strenger

### The Whispering Hillock

- **Where and kind:** Act I, Velen (Downwarren). A main quest when it comes through Ladies of the Wood; found earlier on its own, it shows as a secondary quest.
- **Premise:** The ealdorman says an evil power behind deaths in Downwarren lives in the Whispering Hillock, and the Crones want it gone.
- **How it unfolds:** Under an ancient oak, past a werewolf, is a beating heart in the roots. The spirit says she was a druidess who watched over the land until the Crones murdered her and bound her in the tree, and the killings were nature defending her. She asks to be freed and, if Geralt came from the Crones, promises to save the orphans. **Release:** gather a raven's feather, her bones (Geralt remarks they don't look human) and a wild black horse calmed with Axii; the ritual moves her into the horse. **Trick:** go through the ritual but don't stab the heart, and she dies without a fight. **Fight:** refuse, and break the roots around the heart while endrega workers come in waves. A local book (She Who Knows) tells a different story, that her three daughters killed her because she went mad; the wiki says the truth is never revealed.
- **Consequences (delayed, shown in Return to Crookback Bog):** **Freed:** the spirit saves the orphans (some turn up later at a school in Novigrad), then rides into Downwarren and possesses every villager but the ealdorman, who kill each other. The Crones punish Anna for losing the children by turning her into a water hag. **Killed or tricked:** Downwarren survives (though the witch hunter Graden hangs some villagers for witchcraft), the Crones take the orphans, most likely to eat them, and Anna is found alive but mad. **Edge case** (on the wiki): freed before Geralt has spoken to the ealdorman, Downwarren is still attacked, but Anna is not cursed and the orphans are not actually saved. _(Unverified: a Steam thread says CDPR called this intended.)_
- **Devices:** No right answer: each choice trades one group's lives for another's, and the player can't confirm whose story is true. The thing you were sent to kill may be a victim. The cost lands hours later, in another quest.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Whispering_Hillock ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Ghost_in_the_Tree . Not opened: https://steamcommunity.com/app/292030/discussions/0/490125103635625688

### Return to Crookback Bog

- **Where and kind:** Act I, Velen. Secondary quest that closes the Baron's story; it fails if not done before "The Isle of Mists".
- **Premise:** At the end of Family Matters, Geralt tells the Baron about Anna's pact, and the Baron sets out for the bog to bring her back.
- **How it unfolds:** Downwarren is ravaged or untouched depending on the Hillock. Geralt has to stay near the Baron through the swamp (straying too far fails the quest, and the Baron goes on alone, never seen again). At the orphanage they meet Tamara with Graden's witch hunters. **Hillock killed (or freed early):** Anna is there, mad; after a fight with drowners, hags and a fiend, the Baron decides to take her to a hermit in the Blue Mountains, and Tamara makes him swear to look after her. **Hillock freed after the Crones' task:** Anna is a water hag. Johnny says the Crones cursed her with a lock of her hair; in the cellar Geralt picks one of four dolls. The right one (a violet hollyhock bloom, her favourite flower) lifts the curse, but the curse was what kept her alive and she dies, reconciled with the Baron. The wrong one burns her to death.
- **Consequences:** On the cursed route, either doll ends with Anna dead, and when Geralt goes to Crow's Perch for his pay the Baron has hanged himself in the courtyard. On the mad route, the Baron lives and leaves with Anna, and the orphans are gone. Either way the Baron's men grow crueller to the villagers once he is gone. Nothing done in this quest undoes the Hillock choice.
- **Devices:** The ending is a delayed reveal of a choice made in another quest. A choice inside this quest (the doll) changes only how the set outcome arrives.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Return_to_Crookback_Bog ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_Strenger ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bloody_Baron

### Bald Mountain

- **Where and kind:** Act III, Velen. Main quest, the climax of the Crones' thread.
- **Premise:** After Vesemir's death at Kaer Morhen, Ciri and Geralt go after Imlerith, the Hunt general who killed him, at the Crones' sabbath.
- **How it unfolds:** A local who remembers Geralt served the Ladies points him to a gatekeeper, who admits Ciri but sets Geralt a test (dive for a coin). A sylvan guards the path; Geralt and Ciri play rock-paper-scissors for who gets Imlerith. Ciri (played) kills two of the Crones; the third, Weavess, escapes with Vesemir's medallion. Geralt kills Imlerith in a duel.
- **Consequences:** Weavess's escape is paid off in the tragic ending, where Geralt hunts her down for the medallion. (Anna's story does not come back here; it closed in Return to Crookback Bog.)
- **Devices:** A villain left alive in Act I is paid off in Act III. One Crone escapes, leaving a thread for an ending to pick up.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Bald_Mountain_(quest) ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Something_Ends,_Something_Begins_(quest)

### The Beast of White Orchard

- **Where and kind:** Prologue, White Orchard. Main quest, written as a monster hunt.
- **Premise:** The Nilfgaardian commander will say where Yennefer went only if Geralt brings him the griffin's head.
- **How it unfolds:** The hunter Mislav (after an optional wild-dog hunt) takes Geralt to where a Nilfgaardian patrol was killed; their tracks lead across a broken bridge to the griffin's nest, where Geralt learns _why_ it attacks: the soldiers killed its mate as she slept, and griffins mate for life. The herbalist Tomira points him to buckthorn on a river bed (diving with witcher senses), needed for the hunt _(that it is bait is from summaries, unverified)_. Vesemir has picked an ambush field and hands Geralt a crossbow; shot down, the griffin flees at half health to a ridge by a windmill and is killed there.
- **Consequences:** The commander says Yennefer has gone to Vizima. Geralt can take the pay (150 crowns and XP) or refuse it for more XP.
- **Devices:** The monster has a reason, found by investigation, and the job still gets done. It is the template for later contracts (investigate, read the bestiary, prepare, set a trap, fight). The nest is revisited in the Empress ending.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Beast_of_White_Orchard

### Get Junior

- **Where and kind:** Act I, Novigrad. Main quest, suggested level 12. It follows "Broken Flowers".
- **Premise:** Ciri and Dandelion made a deal with Whoreson Junior, one of Novigrad's crime bosses. Geralt has to find him.
- **How it unfolds:** At Sigi Reuven's (Dijkstra's) bathhouse, Geralt walks in on the other three of the Big Four; Junior's men attack, and afterwards Dijkstra gives three leads in any order: Junior's house (already scrubbed clean by Cleaver's men), his arena and his casino. Each of the last two can be entered by bribe, Axii, a ruse or force (the arena by fighting in it), and ends in a fight. Either one reveals that Junior works for the Redanians, which sends Geralt to Dijkstra, then Roche, then a chess club in Oxenfurt, where the contact turns out to be King Radovid, who says where Junior hides. Following any lead to its end fails the leads not yet done. The optional "The Gangs of Novigrad" (raiding the arena and casino with Cleaver's dwarves) fails once Geralt meets Radovid. Junior's hideout can be entered by a secret passage only if Geralt never asked about Junior or started a fight earlier. Upstairs Geralt finds "a gruesome scene" and Junior, and roughs him up; Ciri's story is told; then **choice:** kill or spare him.
- **Consequences:** The wiki's Get Junior page says the choice "only changes a little flashback later in the game". The Payback page (Ciri's errands in Novigrad, late in the game) shows more: spared, Junior is a beggar in the Bits and Ciri leaves him to his misery; killed, the doppler Dudu has taken his form and turned his business legitimate.
- **Devices:** Three leads open at once that converge on one answer. A side quest that expires when the main quest moves on. How you behaved earlier opens a quiet way in. A mercy choice that pays off in a much later quest.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Get_Junior ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Gangs_of_Novigrad ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Payback

### The King is Dead – Long Live the King, and the Skellige succession

- **Where and kind:** Act I–II, Skellige. A main quest followed by secondary quests: Possession, The Lord of Undvik, King's Gambit, Coronation.
- **Premise:** King Bran's wake opens a succession. Crach an Craite asks Geralt to help his children, Cerys and Hjalmar, each doing a deed to win the jarls' votes.
- **How it unfolds:** **Possession (Cerys):** Jarl Udalryk of Spikeroog hears voices telling him to harm himself. Cerys thinks it is his drowned brother Aki's ghost and wants the family sword returned to Aki's bones; that is done, and Udalryk puts out his own eye anyway. Geralt identifies a hym, a spectre that feeds on guilt. **Choice:** trust Cerys's trick, or the witcher's way (fight the hym in the old house with Udalryk present, calming him with Axii). The trick: Cerys snatches Udalryk's baby and orders Geralt to seal it in a hot oven; Hjort, waiting behind the oven, takes it out safe. The hym jumps to Geralt's guilt, and when the baby is shown alive it has nothing to feed on and leaves. **The Lord of Undvik (Hjalmar):** kill the ice giant of Undvik; Geralt can rescue Hjalmar's companions on the way (Folan among them). **King's Gambit:** opens once both are done. At Crach's feast, bears tear the guests apart; they are berserkers. **Choice:** help Cerys investigate, help Hjalmar, or neither. Cerys's route: spiked mead (mardroeme mushrooms, per the druid Hjort), an attempt on their lives in the cellar, a thread that names the servant Arnvald, a chase. Arnvald (or, if he dies, a hidden letter) gives up a letter signed "The Future King"; before the jarls, Birna denies it, but her son Svanrige says she sent him out of the hall before the attack, and she gives herself away. Hjalmar's route: the Vildkaarls' cave and their druid, whom Hjalmar kills in revenge, so the mastermind is never found; the an Craite name is cleared all the same.
- **Consequences:** The sibling Geralt helps in King's Gambit is crowned (Coronation). Help neither and Svanrige becomes king; let the quest fail and Cerys and Hjalmar later die in battle side by side. Starting King's Gambit locks out "The Cave of Dreams" and "Stranger in a Strange Land". So the earlier help is the entry ticket; the choice that decides the crown is made at the feast.
- **Devices:** An election decided by whose side the player takes. The candidates' characters (cunning or force) shown by how each solves a problem, and the player helps in that style. A case where the evidence isn't enough and a relative's testimony convicts. Cerys's method finds the culprit; Hjalmar's gets revenge and leaves the threat at large.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_King_is_Dead_–_Long_Live_the_King ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Possession_(quest) ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Lord_of_Undvik ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/King%27s_Gambit ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Letter_to_Arnvald ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Birna_Bran

### Through Time and Space

- **Where and kind:** Act III. Main quest, one of four parts of "Final Preparations".
- **Premise:** After Imlerith falls, Avallac'h's plan: Ge'els, one of Eredin's two remaining generals, was loyal to King Auberon, whom Eredin poisoned, so he might withdraw his support.
- **How it unfolds:** The portal is in the cellar of the house where Geralt met Corinne Tilly and Sarah ("Novigrad Dreaming"). Geralt crosses four worlds: a desert, a poisoned valley (where he and Avallac'h are separated), drowned elven ruins and a frozen town with a lighthouse (the Ice Plains). In Tir ná Lia, Avallac'h persuades Ge'els to come and witness, through Corinne Tilly's dreaming, Eredin poisoning Auberon. Ge'els tells them how to lure Eredin and agrees to hold back reinforcements.
- **Devices:** A journey told through set-piece worlds. Turning an enemy lieutenant by showing him the truth about his master.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Through_Time_and_Space

### The endings (Something Ends, Something Begins) and how they are decided

- **Where and kind:** The epilogue of the main quest.
- **How it works:** Three epilogues: Ciri as empress, Ciri as a witcher, and Ciri's death. The wiki lists five hidden points: (1) in Blood on the Battlefield, "I know what might lift your spirits" (the snowball fight) is positive and "Relax, you don't have to be good at everything" negative; (2) if Ciri was taken to Emhyr, refusing his payment is positive and taking it negative; (3) letting Ciri go to the Lodge of Sorceresses alone is positive; (4) "Go for it" when she wrecks Avallac'h's lab is positive, "Calm down" negative; (5) going with her to Skjall's grave is positive. **Witcher:** at least as many positive as negative points; if she was taken to Emhyr, Nilfgaard must also lose the war. **Empress:** three or more positive points, she was taken to Emhyr, and Nilfgaard wins the war, which needs the assassins' quests, a diplomatic Dijkstra in "Blindingly Obvious", and siding with Roche's Temerians in Reason of State (see below). **Tragic:** three or more negative points; Geralt hunts down Weavess for Vesemir's medallion. The epilogue then shows slides of local and political consequences.
- **Scale:** Wikipedia gives "36 possible endings" (citing VG247) and a script of about 450,000 words with 950 speaking roles (citing the voice-casting company). _(Unverified, from summaries: the developers corrected "36 endings" to 36 world states inside three epilogues, with hundreds of smaller variations.)_
- **Devices:** A hidden score. Ordinary parenting moments, never framed as big choices, decide the ending. Side quests decide the fate of a war.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Something_Ends,_Something_Begins_(quest) ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Witcher_3_decision_checklist ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witcher_3:_Wild_Hunt . Not opened: https://www.engadget.com/2013-04-01-the-witcher-3-features-more-than-300-small-variations-in-its-end.html

## Secondary quests

### A Towerful of Mice

- **Where and kind:** Velen, Fyke Isle. Secondary quest, given by Keira Metz. Needed to romance Keira or bring her to Kaer Morhen.
- **Premise:** Lift the curse on Fyke Isle's tower, where Lord Vserad's court was eaten by mice.
- **How it unfolds:** Keira's magic lamp shows the dead re-enacting their last moments; seven hauntings are optional. Logs show the mage Alexander infected rats with the Catriona plague. The ghost of Anabelle, Vserad's daughter, says peasants broke in, she drank what she thought was poison but was a sleeping draught, and rats ate her alive. Geralt reasons that a curse born of hate is broken by love: she must forgive her lover Graham. She asks Geralt to take her bones to Graham. **Choice:** agree, or refuse. The option to refuse appears only if Geralt used the lamp on the optional haunting that shows a looter's murder. **Refuse:** she shows herself as a pesta (plague maiden) and attacks; Geralt brings Graham to the tower, Graham kisses her, she returns to human form and he dies; the curse is lifted and she is at rest. **Take the bones:** Graham admits he let the peasants onto the island; Geralt leaves the bones, hears a scream, and finds Graham dead among rats with Anabelle as a plague maiden, now free to roam with the plague (it almost ruins Kerack).
- **Consequences:** Both endings are tragic, but only refusing keeps the plague maiden bound; that refusal is unlocked by thorough, optional investigation. Keira then asks another favour ("A Favor for a Friend").
- **Devices:** The client (a ghost) is an unreliable witness, and optional investigation is what reveals it and unlocks the better ending. A tool (the lamp) that replays past events. The story is a folk legend (Popiel, the Mouse Tower) retold.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/A_Towerful_of_Mice . Not opened (critical analysis): https://filmstories.co.uk/?p=83020

### For the Advancement of Learning, and Keira's fate (listed in the brief as "Now or Never")

- **Where and kind:** Velen, Fyke Isle. The last of Keira's secondary quests; it starts straight after "A Favor for a Friend", where she drugs Geralt to get at the tower's laboratory. (The brief's "Now or Never" is a different quest: Triss's evacuation of Novigrad's mages, which ends with her departure; see below.)
- **Premise:** Geralt catches Keira leaving the tower with Alexander's notes on the Catriona plague. She means to offer them to King Radovid in return for a pardon for her part in the Lodge.
- **How it unfolds:** **Choices:** let her go to Radovid, persuade her to go to Kaer Morhen, or fight and kill her. The Kaer Morhen option appears once, after the right lines ("Radovid never forgets", "It's suicide"); demanding the notes first, or saying he can't let her go, starts a fight that ends in her death (and Geralt burns the notes). If she agrees to go north, he can ask for the notes without a fight.
- **Consequences (delayed):** **To Kaer Morhen:** she fights in the battle there (she saves Lambert, and they leave together afterwards) and counts towards the "Full Crew" achievement. **To Radovid, or the quest never done:** Radovid has her impaled, which opens "A Final Kindness" in Novigrad; and the later "Brothers In Arms: Velen" fails because she isn't there.
- **Devices:** An ally won or lost by how you talk, with one chance, which shows up in a much later battle. A death you hear about later, which opens a small quest of mourning.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/For_the_Advancement_of_Learning ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Keira_Metz ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/A_Final_Kindness

### A Final Kindness

- **Where and kind:** Novigrad (Hierarch Square), during "Final Preparations" in Act III. A small main quest that exists only if Keira went to Radovid or her quests were never finished.
- **Premise:** Triss tells Geralt that Radovid had Keira impaled, and asks him to help take down her body for burial.
- **How it unfolds:** At night, Geralt deals with the guards on the square (Axii or a fight) and Triss takes down the body; she holds it in a pose the wiki compares to the Pietà.
- **Devices:** A quest that appears only because of an earlier choice in another region, and asks nothing of the player but to help mourn. (The brief's "Pellar" link wasn't confirmed by any page.)
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/A_Final_Kindness

### Carnal Sins

- **Where and kind:** Novigrad. Secondary quest, after "Cabaret".
- **Premise:** Priscilla, a poet and Dandelion's love, is attacked by a serial killer. Geralt investigates.
- **How it unfolds:** The surgeon Joachim von Gratz takes Geralt through the sewers to the morgue to examine the latest victim, a dwarf woodcarver; the coroner Hubert Rejk interrupts and covers for them when Reverend Nathaniel Pastodi, a former Eternal Fire torturer, throws them out. Three leads in any order: the alley where Priscilla was attacked (the killer leapt a wall too high even for a witcher), the woodcarver's workshop, and a corpse collector who sold off a note naming Priscilla as the next victim. Rejk then finds a note naming the next victim, Patricia Vegelbud, who is found murdered; her body names Sweet Nettie at a brothel. There Geralt finds Nathaniel about to torture Nettie. **Choice:** attack him at once, or hear him out. He is sick and cruel but not the killer, and talking to him shows it is Rejk. Geralt then kills Nathaniel, sends him off with Axii, or leaves him; finds Rejk at a dockside warehouse, where he turns out to be a vampire (a katakan, though the wiki's infobox also files him under higher vampires) murdering people he judged sinful in the name of the Eternal Fire.
- **Consequences:** Killing Nathaniel without hearing him out ends the investigation: the real killer can't be found, even if the player has guessed who it is.
- **Devices:** A red herring caught in the act of something vile, and an ending that punishes rushing to judgement. The killer is the person trusted to read the evidence, and the clues against him are in the dialogue.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Carnal_Sins ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Hubert_Rejk

### A Matter of Life and Death

- **Where and kind:** Novigrad. Secondary quest, given by Triss, after "Count Reuven's Treasure".
- **Premise:** Triss is getting Novigrad's mages out of the city. Lady Ingrid Vegelbud offers money for the escape in return for getting her son Albert, a young alchemist the witch hunters are after, out under cover of a masquerade ball.
- **How it unfolds:** Geralt meets her servant at the fish market (an ambush on the way), buys masks at Elihal's (a fox mask for Triss; an optional objective to put on fine clothes). At the ball there are familiar faces, a Gwent tournament and Lady Vegelbud in a parrot mask. A tipsy Triss leads Geralt into the hedge maze. **Choice (timed):** kiss her or not; without the kiss she can't be romanced. Albert and Geralt swap masks on purpose, Triss and Albert leave first, and the witch hunters waiting in the maze take Geralt for Albert and pick a fistfight.
- **Devices:** A social set piece around a rescue. A romance decided in a light moment. A planned decoy: the player wears the target's mask and draws the hunters.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/A_Matter_of_Life_and_Death_(The_Witcher_3)

### The Menge interrogation (Triss and the witch hunters, in Count Reuven's Treasure)

- **Where and kind:** Novigrad. Part of the main quest "Count Reuven's Treasure".
- **Premise:** Dijkstra wants his stolen treasure back, and Dandelion was caught by the witch hunters. Geralt hands Triss over to their leader, Caleb Menge, as a ruse to get inside.
- **How it unfolds:** Menge talks with Geralt while Triss is tortured in the next room. **Choice:** sit through it, or step in (insulting or threatening the guards starts the fight). Sitting through it: Triss frees herself and kills Menge, and the two learn of Dandelion and, if Geralt asked, the treasure's key. Fighting: they search Menge's desk instead and find a spy's letter, a different lead to Dandelion.
- **Consequences:** Either way Menge dies and the trail to Dandelion continues; asking about the treasure decides whether Dijkstra is pleased. _(Unverified: how much the choice colours Triss's later attitude.)_
- **Devices:** A painful wait with a cost in the moment and little lasting effect; the routes give different evidence that reaches the same place.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Count_Reuven%27s_Treasure

### Now or Never

- **Where and kind:** Novigrad. Secondary quest, after "A Favor for Radovid" and "A Matter of Life and Death".
- **Premise:** Triss must get the mages out of Novigrad to Kovir at once.
- **How it unfolds:** Two mages, Berthold and Anisse, haven't reported in. **Choice:** advise Triss to rescue them or not. The rescue saves them, but costs time: the innkeeper Olivier, who is hiding the others, is killed before they arrive. Then the sewers (a katakan) to the docks, where witch hunters attack. **Choice:** say goodbye, or ask Triss to stay and tell her you love her (the romance; with Yennefer also committed, Geralt later loses both in "It Takes Three to Tango").
- **Devices:** A rescue that saves some by being too late for another. A farewell scene that is the romance decision.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Now_or_Never

### Reason of State

- **Where and kind:** Novigrad, Act III. Secondary quest with consequences for the main story. It needs A Deadly Plot completed, An Eye for an Eye and Redania's Most Wanted at least taken to their key points, and Dijkstra bribed with information (not beaten) in "Blindingly Obvious". It fails if "On Thin Ice" starts first.
- **Premise:** Dijkstra, Vernon Roche and Thaler plot to kill King Radovid.
- **How it unfolds:** Geralt lures Radovid out (with Vizimir's ring from Philippa as proof) to a bridge; Radovid orders Geralt killed, Roche's men ambush, and Philippa blinds and stabs Radovid. At the celebration in the theatre, Roche and Thaler reveal a truce with Nilfgaard (Temeria as a vassal); Dijkstra, surrounded by his men, refuses it. **Choice:** walk away and let Roche, Ves and Thaler be killed, or defend them and kill Dijkstra. Refusing the plot fails the quest.
- **Consequences (epilogue):** **Not done:** Radovid wins the war, Emhyr is killed and Temeria ceases to exist. **Dijkstra lives:** he takes the North and wins the war, Emhyr is killed and Temeria is absorbed. **Roche lives:** Nilfgaard wins and Temeria becomes a vassal state. A Nilfgaardian victory is needed for the empress ending.
- **Devices:** A side quest decides the war, and so part of the main ending. A coup whose winners turn on each other, with the player's last choice deciding which.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Reason_of_State ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Witcher_3_decision_checklist

### The Last Wish

- **Where and kind:** Skellige. Secondary quest, Yennefer's. It fails if not done before Geralt fetches Uma from Crow's Perch in "Ugly Baby".
- **Premise:** Yennefer wants a djinn found to undo the wish that once bound her and Geralt, so they can learn whether their love is their own.
- **How it unfolds:** Sailing and diving for the wreck of a mage who studied djinns, then a broken seal and a portal to the other half of the ship on a mountain. The djinn is fought, captured and freed in return for breaking the bond. **Choice:** after Yennefer says nothing has changed for her, tell her you love her (the romance) or that you don't want to be with her.
- **Consequences:** Committing to both Yennefer and Triss leads to "It Takes Three to Tango", where Geralt loses both.
- **Devices:** A test of whether a relationship was ever chosen. Timing: the quest closes when the main story moves on.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Last_Wish_(quest) ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Witcher_3_decision_checklist

### Where the Cat and Wolf Play...

- **Where and kind:** Velen (Honorton, Oreton). A secondary quest (free DLC) that grows out of the contract "The Beast of Honorton". It fails if Geralt leaves the area.
- **Premise:** A beast contract in Honorton.
- **How it unfolds:** The village is dead, killed by a blade, not claws; someone already killed the leshen. A single child, Millie, survived. Geralt follows her doll's trail and wins her trust by giving back the doll or with Axii. She gives him a School of the Cat medallion. The killer is Gaetan, a witcher of that school: after he killed the leshen, the villagers offered a pittance and, when he demanded the agreed fee, tried to kill him; he massacred them, but spared the girl. **Choice:** spare him (opens "Take What You Want", his stash) or kill him. If he asks to drink a Swallow first for a fair fight and Geralt allows it, he uses the moment to throw a blinding bomb. Either way Geralt then takes Millie to her aunt in Oreton (a little coin makes the aunt kinder), and later Millie thanks him with a small gift.
- **Devices:** The contract's "monster" is a fellow witcher. A moral judgement on a peer, with the victims not innocent either. Mercy opens a follow-up quest. A child to see home after the verdict.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Where_the_Cat_and_Wolf_Play...

### Gwent: Big City Players, and High Stakes

- **Where and kind:** Novigrad. Gwent card-game quests.
- **Premise and unfolding:** **Big City Players** is a chain of four opponents, each sending Geralt to the next: Vimme Vivaldi at his bank, Marquise Serenity at the Passiflora, Dijkstra at his bathhouse, and a Scoia'tael merchant outside the city. Each win earns a rare card. Dijkstra is out of reach during parts of the main story. **High Stakes** is a tournament at the Passiflora (1,000 crowns to enter; leaving the area fails it; winning isn't required to finish). Sasha, the strongest player, proposes stealing the prize together, but before Geralt can answer, someone else steals it: Bernard Tulle, a halfling "farmer" and Geralt's first opponent, who is chased down in a dockside warehouse. Sasha turns out to be the Nilfgaardian agent Cantarella, who wanted the money to get out of town; a dinner and a night with her are optional.
- **Devices:** A minigame turned into a social chain. A tournament that becomes a theft, then an exposure.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Gwent:_Big_City_Players ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/High_Stakes

### Fists of Fury (Novigrad, Skellige, and Champion of Champions)

- **Where and kind:** Novigrad, Skellige and Velen. A chain of fistfight quests.
- **How it unfolds:** In Novigrad the fights are Georgius "Piledriver" Georg, Archibald O'Neill, Iron Mortimer, then the champion Durden in Farcorners. Before the Georg bout, Master Claytop offers 80 crowns to throw it (more XP too); refusing may bring thugs later, and agreeing doesn't bind Geralt. In Skellige, Valgard asks Geralt to let him win to impress a woman, and the final opponent is Olaf, a bear. Champion of Champions, after winning all three regions, is in the arena at Hov on Spikeroog, which is closed until "Master of the Arena" is done: the arena is haunted by Ulle the Unlucky, cursed by a jarl never to win a fight, and the curse lifts only if Geralt lets Ulle beat him. The champion is a rock troll.
- **Devices:** A ladder of named opponents. Offers to throw a fight (for money, or for someone's pride). A ghost laid to rest by losing to it.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Fists_of_Fury:_Novigrad ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Fists_of_Fury:_Skellige ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Fists_of_Fury:_Champion_of_Champions ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Master_of_the_Arena

### Wine Wars (Blood and Wine side quests)

- **Where and kind:** Toussaint. A chain of secondary quests.
- **Premise:** Two rival vintners, Liam de Coronata and Matilda Vermentino, each want to buy the Belgaard vineyard, accuse each other of sabotage, and each asks Geralt to clear trouble (monsters) at their estate.
- **How it unfolds and consequences:** Notes found on both estates show someone planted archespores at both, and a key leads to a storehouse ("Wine Wars: The Deus in the Machina"). **Help one and turn it in first:** that one owns Belgaard alone. **Help both but don't investigate before turning in:** they run Belgaard together, grudgingly, and the saboteur is never found. **Help both and investigate first:** a report names the saboteur as Count Vladimir Crespi, the Beast of Beauclair's first victim; the two gladly run Belgaard together, and three days later invite Geralt to name their new wine.
- **Devices:** Working for both sides of a feud reveals a third party. The best outcome needs both jobs _and_ the investigation done before the reward is claimed.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Wine_Wars:_Belgaard ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Wine_Wars:_The_Deus_in_the_Machina

## Witcher contracts

### Contract: Devil by the Well

- **Where and kind:** White Orchard. The first contract.
- **Premise:** Odolan's daughter is ill from the corpse-fouled river, and the only clean water is a well in an abandoned village, haunted by a noonwraith.
- **How it unfolds:** In a hut, a diary by a woman named Claer and a skeleton; blood leads outside. At the end of the well's bucket rope hangs a woman's skeleton; Geralt concludes her bracelet fell into the well and dives for it. Burning the skeleton and bracelet summons her at any hour; Yrden makes her vulnerable, and she splits into three to heal. Odolan's pay can be refused for an amethyst, and the herbalist Tomira tells Claer's story if asked.
- **Devices:** The monster is a murdered woman. An object ties the ghost here, and destroying it ends the haunting. The backstory is optional, told by a third party afterwards.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Contract:_Devil_by_the_Well

### Contract: Jenny o' the Woods

- **Where and kind:** Velen (Midcopse). Contract.
- **Premise:** Ealdorman Bolko posts a bounty for a monster in the fields.
- **How it unfolds:** The witness Agneta fled at once, but says it came after twilight. A torn cloth and tracks lead to a pool of blood with a knife, then to a shallow grave with a letter. The ealdorman works out that the dead woman is Zula, who meant to run away with her lover Ontan; Bokhai, whom she had refused, followed and killed her. She has become the nightwraith. At the lovers' meeting place, Geralt burns Zula's letter and the dried blood from the knife to summon her at night. She vanishes and sends three lesser nightwraiths, healing while they live; Yrden, specter oil and moon dust help. She drops a nightwraith trophy and a Forgotten Vran Sword.
- **Devices:** The monster is a murder victim, and the murder is solved as part of the contract. The murder weapon and the letter are the summoning. (The names in the brief, Gretka and Erland of Larvik, belong to no page opened.)
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Contract:_Jenny_o%27_the_Woods ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Jenny_o%27_the_Woods

### Contract: Woodland Beast

- **Where and kind:** Velen (Border Post). Contract.
- **Premise:** The guard captain says a "shaggy" beast is attacking Redanian transports on the roads.
- **How it unfolds:** At the attack site, alghouls are feeding on the dead; witcher senses show looted chests and a body against a fine cart, and the other bodies only show that necrophages fed on them. _(That the wounds were made after death is the first pass's wording, unverified.)_ Tracks lead past drowners to a Scoia'tael band in the trees. **Choice:** ask for their commander and surrender your weapons (refuse, or answer otherwise, and they attack). Their commander Vernossiel says they kill Redanian soldiers, who tortured and killed theirs, and need the supplies. Then: let them be, or kill them (with your weapons, after first agreeing, or barehanded with a sword picked up on the spot).
- **Consequences:** Kill them: show a squirrel tail for a letter of safe conduct and 30 crowns. Spare them and report them: the letter only, and Vernossiel may later find Geralt and attack him for the betrayal. Spare them and say there was no monster: nothing but a little XP. Spared, they return his weapons with a few items.
- **Devices:** The "monster" is a cover for people. The evidence contradicts the client's story. Who you report is a moral choice with a material reward and a later cost.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Contract:_Woodland_Beast

### Contract: The Merry Widow

- **Where and kind:** Velen (Lindenvale). Contract.
- **Premise:** The gravedigger reports bodies dug up and a young child missing.
- **How it unfolds:** Rabid dogs at the church; skeletons dug up without tools; a cadaver's scent leads over a broken bridge to a hut with a cauldron and skulls, which tell Geralt it is a grave hag. He takes the skulls back to the church as a lure, and at night Mourntart appears.
- **Devices:** The plain investigate, identify, lure and kill shape. A missing child raises the stakes, and the gravedigger asks about the child at the end.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Contract:_The_Merry_Widow

### Contract: Shrieker

- **Where and kind:** Velen (Crow's Perch). Contract, suggested level 8.
- **Premise:** Chet, who posted it, says Borzoy, the old lord's huntsman, died trying to kill a beast alone; Chet has taken in Borzoy's orphaned son Symko.
- **How it unfolds:** Symko, in shock, says a little (Axii gets more). A dead dog, tracks and monster blood lead past the burned ruins to a cave, which tells Geralt it is a cockatrice. A bomb drives it out of its hiding place, it flees out the back, and Geralt chases it down. **Choice at payment:** take the coin, or tell Chet to keep it for Symko (XP only); then Geralt may tell Symko his father is avenged.
- **Devices:** An earlier hunter's failure is the backstory. The reward can be given to the dead hunter's child. (No scam or hotel twist exists; the brief may be mixing it with another quest.)
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Contract:_Shrieker

### Contract: The Griffin from the Highlands

- **Where and kind:** Velen (Crow's Perch notice board). Contract, linked to "Master Armorers".
- **Premise:** An archgriffin in the hills; it appears only once the armourer Yoana needs archgriffin acid.
- **How it unfolds:** Follow a blood trail and a feather uphill, kill it, take the acid gland for Yoana and the trophy to the quartermaster at Crow's Perch.
- **Devices:** A contract whose loot opens a crafting quest. (The brief's "mate killed" investigation belongs to the prologue's "The Beast of White Orchard", above.)
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Contract:_The_Griffin_from_the_Highlands

### Contract: Missing Miners

- **Where and kind:** Skellige (Blandare, Ard Skellig). Contract, free DLC.
- **Premise:** Gjarr says young men from the village went north looking for silver and haven't come back.
- **How it unfolds:** Bodies and tracks lead to a cave and a rock troll, Wham-a-Wham, who attacks. Once he is worn down (or if Geralt runs from him, or uses Axii), he talks: the miners came into his home, he asked them to leave, they kept hammering at the silver, so he killed them. **Choice:** let it slide (he promises not to do it again) or kill him.
- **Consequences:** Spared, the troll gives Geralt a little coin and silver ore; Gjarr admits they had heard of a troll and went anyway, is annoyed, and pays less. Killed, Gjarr pays the full fee, and the troll yields a trophy, a mutagen and the relic sword "Caroline".
- **Devices:** A contract that can end without a kill. The monster was defending its home from greed the client knew about. Mercy costs money.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Contract:_Missing_Miners

### In Wolf's Clothing (the Morkvarg contract; listed in the brief as "The Beast of Honorfall")

- **Where and kind:** Skellige (Hindarsfjall: Larvik and Freya's Garden). Secondary quest from the notice "Contract: Morkvarg", or through the main quests "Missing Persons" and "Nameless".
- **Premise:** The priestess Josta says Morkvarg, a pirate who desecrated Freya's Garden, was cursed into a werewolf by the dying archpriestess and keeps coming back after being killed.
- **How it unfolds:** Einar, a "pilgrim" who survived the raid, tells the story, but something in it is off. Morkvarg can be cut down and talked to (he asks for the curse to be lifted, promising a reward), and drops his own flesh. **Route 1:** feed him his own flesh; the curse forbids it, and he dies. **Route 2:** a key found by opening floodgates leads to Morkvarg's journal, which shows Einar was one of his crew. Pressed (or fought), Einar admits he laid the curse himself, by cutting Morkvarg with a cursed fang, and the fang lifts it. **Choices after lifting it:** kill the human Morkvarg (as Einar asks), or let him go and claim his reward (a sword from a Novigrad loan shark).
- **Consequences:** Lifting the curse and sparing Morkvarg counts as one example of mercy in "Skellige's Most Wanted" (below).
- **Devices:** The witness lied, and research exposes him. A curse lifted through research, or ended by a cruel loophole. Mercy shown to a monster here is remembered in another contract.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/In_Wolf%27s_Clothing

### Contract: Skellige's Most Wanted

- **Where and kind:** Skellige (Fyresdal). Contract, free DLC.
- **Premise:** A man (who muddles his geography and clan colours) begs a witcher to clear nekkers that attacked his cart.
- **How it unfolds:** The attack site doesn't add up, a boulder nearly crushes Geralt, a cave entrance closes behind him, and an endrega nest waits inside: it is a trap. Crude drawings in the cave plan Geralt's death and name a meeting place. At night four monsters arrive: a godling, an ice troll, a doppler (the "client") and a werewolf who wants witchers to pay for his mate's death. **Choice:** fight, try to part ways (both lead to a fight with all but the godling), or prove you have helped monsters before. Proof needs examples from two different kinds of monster. Accepted examples: sparing the trolls at Kaer Morhen, letting Sarah stay in Novigrad, sparing the succubus Salma (Deadly Delights), sparing Wham-a-Wham (Missing Miners), sparing an imp (An Elusive Thief), sparing a god (A Greedy God), lifting Morkvarg's curse and sparing him, helping Johnny get his voice back (two godlings count once). With proof, the others back down; the werewolf, blinded by hatred, attacks and must be killed, and the godling thanks Geralt.
- **Devices:** The player's record of mercy across earlier quests becomes the argument in a confrontation. A partial peace (all but the leader). A contract that is itself the monsters' trap.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Contract:_Skellige%27s_Most_Wanted

### Contract: Deadly Delights

- **Where and kind:** Novigrad. Contract, posted by the Temple Guard.
- **Premise:** Guardsmen are being found dead.
- **How it unfolds:** A guard and a medic at the docks describe the bodies; behind the brothel Crippled Kate's, witcher senses find two-legged hoofprints and a perfume trail. Either Axii on the madam (who names a succubus and hands over a key) or the scent (to a drained man, then over the rooftops into the house) leads to the succubus Salma. Heard out, she says the guards surprised and attacked her, and she defended herself. **Choice:** kill her, or make her leave the city.
- **Consequences:** Sent away, she hands over her trophy as proof and a sword; killed, she yields the trophy, a rare mutagen and gauntlets. The guard pays either way. Sparing her counts in "Skellige's Most Wanted".
- **Devices:** The "killer monster" says she acted in self-defence, and the journal accepts her account if she is spared. Two investigation routes (force a witness, or follow the evidence). Mercy versus loot.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Contract:_Deadly_Delights

### Contract: The Tufo Monster (Blood and Wine)

- **Where and kind:** Toussaint (Tufo vineyard). Contract.
- **Premise:** Jean-Christophe de Bourbeau has noises in his cellar and a missing worker, Jean-Luc.
- **How it unfolds:** The cellar wall hides kikimore tunnels (burning the eggs earns extra pay). His wife Andrée describes where Jean-Luc vanished; the clues there show the two were picnicking, not working. Tracks lead to a cave; Geralt makes shaelmaar bait from the freshest corpse (Jean-Luc's), and finds his silver pendant beside it. The blind shaelmaar is beaten by baiting it into walls with Aard. **Choice:** give the pendant to the husband (exposing the affair; he throws his wife out) or to the wife (keeping her secret; it pays slightly more and counts as "proof of compassion" in the side quest "There Can Be Only One"), or keep it.
- **Devices:** A domestic secret found during a monster hunt. Here discretion pays a little better than exposure.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Contract:_The_Tufo_Monster

### Big Game Hunter (Blood and Wine)

- **Where and kind:** Toussaint. A contract from the notice "Wanted: The White Wolf", posted on almost every board.
- **How it unfolds:** Count Beledal, a rich Koviri noble, hires Geralt as a guide to find animals and "preserve" their images, and doesn't want them hurt: keep away from a bear, free a panther from a trap (Axii her first), draw giant centipedes out without hurting them, and Axii peacocks into spreading their tails. A centipede follows and must be killed. His men reveal that his daughter is disabled, and he travels to bring her pictures of the world. The next day he shows paintings made from the images and gives Geralt one.
- **Devices:** The contract form reversed: the client wants the creatures protected, not killed. A small reveal that reframes an eccentric client.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Big_Game_Hunter

## Small quests and "question marks"

### Twisted Firestarter

- **Where and kind:** White Orchard. Small secondary quest (not Novigrad, as the brief had it). It fails once the prologue ends.
- **Premise:** Willis, a dwarven smith whose forge burned down just before Geralt arrived, suspects arson.
- **How it unfolds:** Tracks lead to the river, where a drowner wounded the arsonist, and back into the village to Napp, hiding with an injured hand. He offers 20 crowns to drop it. **Choice:** take the bribe, or bring him in (by fistfight or Axii).
- **Consequences:** Turned in, Napp is taken by Nilfgaardian soldiers friendly with Willis, sentenced and executed on the spot. Either way Willis becomes a smith Geralt can use.
- **Devices:** A tiny murder-mystery shape (a crime, a trail, a confrontation, a verdict) on an errand for a craftsman, whose reward is a service. The verdict is harsher than the player may expect.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Twisted_Firestarter

### Fake Papers, with Bitter Harvest and Thou Shalt Not Pass

- **Where and kind:** Velen (Border Post). A small chain.
- **Premise:** Radovid has closed the Pontar crossings; guards let through only those with a pass.
- **How it unfolds:** "Thou Shalt Not Pass" names three ways to a pass: the Baron's letter of safe conduct (Family Matters), the letter from the Woodland Beast contract, or a fake from a shady merchant ("Fake Papers"). The fake costs 100 crowns, 50 after Axii, or 25 if Geralt helps the merchant's brother-in-law, Albin Hart, whose crew loot a battlefield while Geralt holds off waves of necrophages ("Bitter Harvest"). One can also just swim across.
- **Consequences:** If Albin dies and Geralt says so, the merchant refuses to deal and Fake Papers fails; keeping quiet about it and paying one of the other prices still gets a pass.
- **Devices:** A price that depends on whether someone you helped lived, and on whether you admit it. Several quests offering different routes to one goal (a favour from a lord, a report, a bribe, a forger).
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Fake_Papers ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Bitter_Harvest ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Thou_Shalt_Not_Pass

### Funeral Pyres

- **Where and kind:** Velen. Small secondary quest.
- **Premise:** A priest of the Eternal Fire asks Geralt to burn the dead in three pits with holy oil.
- **How it unfolds:** Each pit is guarded by necrophages. At the last, a man, Titus, is fighting off ghouls; saved and asked, he says he dealt fisstech, the priest was his customer, the priest tried to kill him at their last trade, and hired a witcher to burn the evidence.
- **Consequences:** Without Titus's story, Geralt just takes his pay. With it, he can take the priest's bribe, or refuse and kill the priest and his guards (and find more coin on the body).
- **Devices:** A grim chore that turns out to be the client covering a crime. A rescue on the job supplies the evidence. A choice at payment.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Funeral_Pyres

## Hearts of Stone

### Evil's Soft First Touches

- **Where and kind:** No Man's Land and Oxenfurt. The expansion's first main quest.
- **Premise:** A man nails a new contract to the Seven Cats Inn board as Geralt watches: Olgierd von Everec, at the Garin estate, wants a monster in the Oxenfurt sewers killed, rumoured to be a prince turned into a toad.
- **How it unfolds:** Olgierd's band may pick a fistfight, avoidable with the right reply. Haggling raises the pay to 450 crowns. In the sewers Geralt meets Shani again, and finds a key on a dead soldier and the way down. The Toad Prince is a hard, mandatory fight. Dying, it turns back into a man, and Ofieri soldiers who had sworn to bring their prince home seize Geralt to be executed in Ofir. On the ship, Gaunter O'Dimm appears and offers help in return for a favour; Geralt agrees, and O'Dimm burns a mark onto his face. A storm wrecks the ship and Geralt escapes. At a crossroads at night O'Dimm explains: Olgierd knew who the toad was, and Olgierd owes O'Dimm too, but before collecting, O'Dimm must grant Olgierd three wishes through a proxy, who is Geralt. The Garin estate is burning when Geralt returns; Olgierd turns out to be immortal (he survives a beheading if Geralt fights him) and names the first two wishes. Leaving the estate can open "Rose on a Red Field".
- **Consequences:** Geralt is bound into O'Dimm's pact with Olgierd for the rest of the expansion.
- **Devices:** A routine contract that is a trap set by the client. A three-way pact. A rescue that is really a purchase.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Evil%27s_Soft_First_Touches

### Gaunter O'Dimm in the base game (foreshadowing)

- **Where and kind:** Prologue, White Orchard, during "Lilac and Gooseberries". Not a quest of its own.
- **What happens:** At the White Orchard inn, a pleasant vagrant, Gaunter O'Dimm, buys Geralt a drink, says he knows of him and Yennefer from Dandelion's ballads, passes on rumours, sends him to the Nilfgaardian garrison, and declines any reward because "one day he may need Geralt's help", then is gone. Talking to him is what completes the objective. He returns as the antagonist of Hearts of Stone ("Master Mirror", "the Man of Glass"), and reminds Geralt of this first meeting.
- **Devices:** A villain planted many hours before his story, in a moment that seems to help, with the debt named out loud and passed off as a pleasantry.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Gaunter_O%27Dimm ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Lilac_and_Gooseberries

### Olgierd's three wishes (structure)

- **Structure:** Olgierd's first two "impossible" wishes, named together: show his dead brother Vlodimir the time of his life (Dead Man's Party), and bring him Maximilian Borsodi's house (Open Sesame!). They can be done in either order. Only when both are done does he give the third, kept back until then: bring him the violet rose he gave his wife Iris the last time he saw her (Scenes From a Marriage).
- **Devices:** A list of tasks with a hidden last item. Each wish is a quest in a different genre (a comic possession at a wedding, a heist, a ghost story).
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Evil%27s_Soft_First_Touches ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Open_Sesame!_(Hearts_of_Stone) ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Scenes_From_a_Marriage

### Dead Man's Party

- **Where and kind:** Hearts of Stone. Main quest (a wish).
- **Premise:** Show Olgierd's long-dead brother Vlodimir a good time.
- **How it unfolds:** Shani's book on Redanian families locates the von Everec crypt. A censer ritual raises the family's ghosts, who attack; then Vlodimir appears and possesses Geralt. Shani suggests the wedding she invited Geralt to. Vlodimir (in Geralt's body, in borrowed clothes and a new haircut) runs the party: herding pigs, a fight with a girl's brothers, Gwent, finding a drunk fire-swallower, fishing out Shani's shoe. O'Dimm turns up and dedicates a song. Vlodimir promised to keep his hands off Shani once he learned what she means to Geralt, but at midnight he breaks his word and tries to stay; O'Dimm banishes him. Geralt delivers Vlodimir's letter in blood to Olgierd.
- **Consequences:** The banishment opens the optional "A Midnight Clear" (the moment with Shani), which fails if Geralt leaves the wedding first.
- **Devices:** The protagonist is possessed by a comic ghost, a change of tone in a dark story. A romance woven into the set piece. A tie-in you lose by leaving the area.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Dead_Man%27s_Party

### Open Sesame! (the heist)

- **Where and kind:** Hearts of Stone, Oxenfurt. Main quest (a wish).
- **Premise:** Bring Olgierd "Maximilian Borsodi's house"; Olgierd gives no location or means.
- **How it unfolds:** Vivaldi gets Geralt into the Borsodi auction house as his guest (art, gear and Gwent side-challenges, an auction). Horst Borsodi has him thrown out, and a stranger sends him to a herbalist's hut outside town with a password. The stranger plans to rob the vault and needs Geralt to assemble the crew. Three sub-quests: hire a safecracker (**Quinto**, quiet, or **Casimir**, explosives), recruit a climber (Eveline Gallo), and bribe a cook to spike the guards' food. During the heist the house is not empty; Geralt negotiates with guards outside while the safecracker works. With Quinto, the right answers avoid any fight, but he misses a trapdoor into a pit of arachnomorphs; with Casimir, an explosion brings the guards in, but it also wrecks the trap. At the vault the stranger is revealed as Ewald Borsodi, Horst's cheated brother. **Choice:** side with Ewald or Horst (staying neutral is refused). Quinto sides with Horst, Casimir with Ewald, so Geralt may fight his own safecracker. The "house" is a golden casket holding their father's will. Last choice: a deal (take the casket, leave the papers) or fight the surviving brother for everything.
- **Consequences:** The main quest goes on either way. _(Unverified, from a review summary: the planning choices add flavour more than real freedom.)_
- **Devices:** A heist built by recruiting specialists, each choice changing a later obstacle, and whose loyalties come out mid-job. The impossible object turns out to be an ordinary thing with an odd name. The quest-giver has his own family grudge.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Open_Sesame!_(Hearts_of_Stone)

### Scenes From a Marriage

- **Where and kind:** Hearts of Stone. Main quest (the third wish).
- **Premise:** Bring Olgierd the violet rose he once gave his wife Iris.
- **How it unfolds:** At the abandoned von Everec manor, a thief's partner has been killed by the Caretaker, an eyeless monster with a spade that heals itself with each hit. A talking black cat and dog send Geralt inside, past a wraith that moves between paintings, to Iris's long-dead body. Geralt buries her (choosing a keepsake for the grave) and follows her ghost into a painted world of the manor. There he completes seven frozen memories (eight with an optional one) by putting missing objects back, re-living the marriage's decay: her family's disapproval, Olgierd's failed attempt to summon O'Dimm and break the pact, his growing indifference, the cat, dog and Caretaker he left her for company and protection. His farewell letter summons her greatest fear, ethereal copies of Olgierd. **Choice:** Iris holds the rose, and her existence is bound to it. Leave it (she sleeps on; Geralt brings Olgierd a painting of her with the rose instead), or take it (she gives it up, the painted world crumbles, and the freed cat and dog warn Geralt to "seek salvation in glass that can't be broken" if O'Dimm stands in his way).
- **Consequences:** Either object completes the wish. Taking the rose gets the hint that helps in the final wager. _(Unverified, from a review summary: the choice "does not offer a clean rescue".)_
- **Devices:** A ghost story told as a walk through a marriage's memories, solved by restoring them. The "impossible" task means ending someone's afterlife, and mercy and usefulness point different ways.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Scenes_From_a_Marriage

### Whatsoever a Man Soweth... (ending)

- **Where and kind:** Hearts of Stone. The final main quest.
- **Premise:** With the wishes granted, O'Dimm comes for Olgierd's soul at a temple whose floor is a mosaic of the moon (their contract said "on the moon").
- **How it unfolds:** An optional step: Shani asks to see Geralt, and takes him to Professor Shakeslock at the Oxenfurt Academy, who explains O'Dimm can be beaten only in a game of wits with your soul at stake, then dies in an accident. Skip that visit and Geralt can only watch O'Dimm take Olgierd. **Choice (if he met Shakeslock):** let O'Dimm take Olgierd, or wager both souls. The wager is a riddle against the clock in a twisted otherworld full of decoys; the answer is a reflection that can't be broken, found by filling a dry fountain (Aard on a cracked wall) so O'Dimm shows in the water.
- **Consequences:** Let O'Dimm win and he offers a wish: speed (a horse caparison), never going hungry, an always-full bottle, riches, or advice about Ciri (O'Dimm says finding her is beyond him), or nothing. Win, and O'Dimm must leave; Olgierd gives Geralt his sabre, Iris. (The Viper silver sword lies in the wager's otherworld, so it is not tied to an ending, as the first pass said.)
- **Devices:** A devil's bargain beaten by information gathered earlier and optionally (Shakeslock's rule; the cat and dog's hint). A riddle as the final boss. A reward that tempts the player to pick the worse ending.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Whatsoever_a_Man_Soweth... ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Witcher_3_decision_checklist

## Blood and Wine

### The Beast of Toussaint (the Beast of Beauclair)

- **Where and kind:** Toussaint (Beauclair). Main quest that opens the expansion.
- **Premise:** The knights Milton de Peyrac-Peyran and Palmerin de Launfal bring Geralt to Toussaint, where Duchess Anna Henrietta wants a beast that is killing knights stopped.
- **How it unfolds:** A giant at a windmill, then the latest victim's scene by a river (an embroidered handkerchief names him), a bruxa at the Corvo Bianco cellar where the body was taken, a blind shaelmaar at the tourney (sparing it is optional). Pooling what they know, Geralt and the duchess see that the victims were once a team, and the next will be Milton, who is playing the hare in a court game. Breaking the game's rules to win its clues, they find him in a greenhouse, dead, with the killer standing over him; the killer escapes. _(Unverified, from a Prima chapter title: the five knightly virtues are part of the link.)_
- **Consequences:** In the following quests the killer turns out to be Dettlaff, a higher vampire and friend of Geralt's old companion Regis, who acted for someone he loved; the one manipulating him is Syanna (Sylvia Anna), the duchess's exiled sister. _(The details of how she manipulated him are from summaries and weren't checked on a quest page.)_
- **Devices:** The "beast" is a person being used by another. The victims are linked in a way only investigation finds. The client's own family is behind it.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Beast_of_Toussaint ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Dettlaff_van_der_Eretein

### La Cage au Fou (before the fork)

- **Where and kind:** Toussaint. A main quest after "Blood Run", well before the route choice (the first pass put it in the wrong place).
- **How it unfolds:** To find Dettlaff, Regis brews "Resonance". It needs the saliva of a spotted wight, thought extinct: Geralt can kill the wight, or hide, take the saliva from her cauldron, follow her and lift her curse, turning her back into Marlene, a woman cursed centuries before. The last ingredient is a higher vampire's blood in a state of great suffering, so Regis lets his bloodlust build while Geralt fights off monsters at the cemetery.
- **Devices:** A monster that can be cured rather than killed, discovered only by holding back. A friend pays a personal price.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/La_Cage_au_Fou

### The Night of Long Fangs, Blood Simple and the route choice

- **Where and kind:** Toussaint. Main quests in the middle of the expansion.
- **How it unfolds:** Dettlaff, as he threatened, sets vampires on Beauclair. There are two ways to reach him: find Captain Damien, who knows where the duchess hid Syanna (the Land of a Thousand Fables, "Beyond Hill and Dale..."), or find the bruxa Orianna, who knows how to reach the Unseen Elder, the eldest vampire, who can compel Dettlaff to come ("What Lies Unseen"). In "Blood Simple", the orphanage Orianna runs has been attacked; Geralt tracks the killer, an alpha garkain, and kills it in return for her help. A letter on a body and her own behaviour show that Orianna feeds on the orphans' blood (without killing them). Geralt lets her go for now, vowing to come back for her, and gets the key to the Elder's cave.
- **The fork:** both leads can be followed for a while. The choice is fixed only by opening the Land of a Thousand Fables book, or by telling Regis at the docks that you want the Unseen Elder. At the Elder's cave, a wrong word gets Geralt killed outright.
- **Consequences:** The Unseen Elder route is shorter and ends with both sisters dead (see the endings). The reckoning with Orianna isn't in the expansion; the wiki's Orianna page treats the 2015 cinematic trailer "A Night to Remember" as that later meeting. _(The first pass's "a fan mod added one" is unverified.)_
- **Devices:** The ally's charity is a front for predation, and the reckoning is promised, not delivered. A fork that looks like a choice of method but decides the ending, committed by one line of dialogue.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Night_of_Long_Fangs ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Blood_Simple ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Orianna ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/What_Lies_Unseen ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Witcher_3_decision_checklist

### Beyond Hill and Dale... (the Land of a Thousand Fables)

- **Where and kind:** Toussaint, inside a magical fairy-tale world made long ago for the ducal sisters and entered from the palace playroom. Main quest.
- **Premise:** Find Syanna, hidden in the fairy-tale world, which is decaying and turning hostile.
- **How it unfolds:** Geralt finds Syanna at the Wicked Witch's hut. The only way out is up a beanstalk, so they need three beans; Joss, the boy who cried wolf, gives clues that reverse the truth. The red bean is inside the Big Bad Wolf, who won't play his part because Little Red Riding Hood is dead, so Syanna wears the dead girl's hood. The yellow is in Longlocks's tower, the blue with the Three Little Pigs. Between beans, Syanna offers to tell her story; listening (instead of rejecting her) is one of the conditions for the best ending. The Little Flint Girl, selling drugs in a snowy village, wears a ribbon Syanna was given as a child for protection; it can be had for 500 crowns or a game of Gwent. The governess's journal, from the playroom in the previous quest, tells of Syanna's childhood; reading its first two entries is another condition.
- **Consequences:** The ribbon saves Syanna's life at Tesham Mutna (it teleports her to safety). The ribbon, the journal and listening to her are three of the conditions for the best ending.
- **Devices:** An optional object, easy to miss and found in a whimsical side space, decides life and death in the finale. The villain's backstory is optional reading and optional listening, which the player must have done to sway her later.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Beyond_Hill_and_Dale... ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Witcher_3_decision_checklist ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Pomp_and_Strange_Circumstance

### Tesham Mutna, Pomp and Strange Circumstance, and the endings

- **Where and kind:** Toussaint. The finale.
- **How it unfolds:** At Tesham Mutna, if Geralt brought Syanna, Dettlaff tries to kill her. **With the ribbon:** she vanishes to safety, and an enraged Dettlaff must be fought. **Without it:** she dies, and Geralt can fight Dettlaff or let him go (Dettlaff vows to stay away from people). **Unseen Elder route:** Dettlaff, compelled to come, attacks at once. Beaten, Dettlaff is killed by Regis. In "Pomp and Strange Circumstance" (only if Syanna lived), Regis wonders who the fifth victim was meant to be; investigating (a bootblack, a beggars' shelter) shows it was the duchess herself. Geralt can then talk to Syanna in her tower; with the right lines ("Cause she turned her back on you, forgot about you", then "Ever thought to forgive her?") and every earlier condition met, she forgives her sister at the ceremony.
- **Consequences:** **Best:** both sisters live and are reconciled. Needs the Fables route, the ribbon, the governess's journal read, Syanna's story heard, the fifth victim investigated and the right words to her; missing any of these leads to both deaths. **Both die:** Syanna kills Anna Henrietta at the ceremony and is shot by Damien (also the result of the Unseen Elder route). **Syanna dies at Tesham Mutna:** the duchess has Geralt jailed ("Burlap is the New Stripe"), and letting Dettlaff go costs Geralt his fee.
- **Devices:** Several small, optional, easily missed acts (an item, reading, listening, a last investigation) add up to the best ending, and missing one gives the worst. A sympathetic killer whose death is required even in the best ending. A friend (Regis) is the one who has to kill him.
- **Sources:** https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Tesham_Mutna ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Pomp_and_Strange_Circumstance ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Dettlaff_van_der_Eretein ; https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Witcher_3_decision_checklist

## How CD Projekt Red designs quests (what developers and critics have said)

None of the press articles behind this section could be opened (the proxy refused every press and guide site). What is confirmed comes from Wikipedia's articles, which cite those sources; the rest is from search summaries and says so.

- **Confirmed (Wikipedia, "The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt", citing Eurogamer, Kotaku and Glixel):** each character was given a unique personality "to contrast the fetch-quest system typically used in video games"; early in development each side quest's dialogue was limited to 15 lines, and writers had to argue for more; player options were written to be morally ambiguous; alcoholism, abuse and sexuality were written in as normal parts of the world. The script ran to about 450,000 words with 950 speaking roles. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witcher_3:_Wild_Hunt
- **Confirmed (Wikipedia, "The Bloody Baron"):** writers outline what happens and designers outline what the player does, then the teams finalise it together. At GDC 2017 Sasko showed that a quest's story is mapped to a pacing graph of tension per scene and beat, alongside its combat and other play; his team avoids clichés unless used knowingly and often throws away first ideas. The Baron quest began as one sentence, was meant to embody Velen, and was partly drawn from Sasko's childhood in a poor mountain village. Sasko and Stachyra wanted no one wholly good or wholly evil; the choice to forgive the Baron was added so players could form their own view (most forgave him, which Sasko took as validation); Stachyra said stories need not end happily and Geralt should not always find a perfect solution. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bloody_Baron
- **Open world and consequence** _(unverified, summary of GameSpot)_: Philipp Weber, quoting Mateusz Tomaszkiewicz, said the aim was to keep the earlier games' "complex and mature story that has choices and consequences" in an open world, with quests that work when approached by different routes; doing parts out of order could have later consequences, "a huge bonus". https://www.gamespot.com/articles/cd-projekt-red-explains-why-the-witcher-3s-open-wo/1100-6477549/
- **No fetch quests; every quest has a hook** _(unverified beyond the Wikipedia line above)_: summaries report the studio wanting no "FedEx" quests and every quest, "even the very tiny ones", having "some kind of story, tease, twist, rumor"; Jakub Szamałek: when there are fetch quests, "we compensate for it with the narrative"; Tomaszkiewicz admitting some quests "felt padded; like fetch quests" despite the rule; Philipp Weber saying the rule carries on. https://comicbook.com/comicbook/news/the-witcher-3-developers-promise-you-wont-find-two-identical-sid/ ; https://stevivor.com/features/interviews/cd-projekt-reds-mateusz-tomaszkiewicz-on-the-witcher-3-wild-hunt-and-lessons-learned-for-hearts-of-stone/ ; https://attackofthefanboy.com/news/cd-projekt-red-eliminating-fetch-quests-witcher-3/ ; https://gamingbolt.com/the-witcher-4-wont-have-fetch-quests-assures-narrative-director
- **No obvious right answer** _(unverified; the source wasn't reachable)_: Marcin Blacha, story director, reportedly said a great quest makes the player stop and weigh the decision, the choice should never have an obvious right answer, and it should make players reflect on people and how hard life can be. The Wikipedia lines on moral ambiguity and on the Baron's forgiveness choice support the spirit, not the quote. https://www.notebookcheck.net/The-Witcher-3-story-director-explains-what-makes-a-truly-great-quest.1400273.0.html
- **Hold back information** _(unverified; the source wasn't reachable)_: summaries of Sasko's GDC 2023 talk "10 Key Quest Design Lessons" list: beware of overdesign ("a game is not a life simulation"); hold back key information and let the player uncover it ("absence of information"); make emotion a core pillar and never rush an emotional scene; try topics nobody has done; be brief. The full list wasn't seen. https://gdcvault.com/play/1028897/10-Key-Quest-Design-Lessons ; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/10-key-takeaways-from-the-quest-design-of-cyberpunk-2077-and-the-witcher-3 ; https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20230408-key-lessons-from-pawel-sasko
- **About 40-minute beats** _(unverified; the source wasn't reachable)_: Sasko reportedly said "in the Bloody Baron quest, I made so many mistakes": almost four hours long and badly paced; the studio's rule from the expansions on is focused beats of about 40 minutes of intense, themed play and story, then open-world breathing room, broken on purpose when it helps. The only checked fact near this is the GDC 2017 pacing graph above. https://kotaku.com/a-witcher-3-dev-reflects-on-its-most-memorable-quest-the-bloody-baron-quest-i-made-so-many-mistakes-2000730612 ; https://ixbt.games/en/news/2026/09/03/432255-odin-iz-lucsix-kvestov-the-witcher-3-teper-smushhaet-razrabotcika-on-priznal-cto-dopustil-kucu-osibok.html
- **Other claims from summaries, unverified:** Konrad Tomaszkiewicz on CDPR being "all about evoking emotion" (TechRaptor); the 2016 forum Q&A on the pipeline (pitches trimmed, scenarios revised or scrapped, placeholder first drafts, the designer keeping the mood through lighting, music and animation); a 40-page design document for the Baron before any dialogue (GamePro); Sasko proposing Vesemir's death and the Battle of Kaer Morhen being the hardest quest to make; Game Informer quoting Stachyra on a story where "everything is ambiguous"; Filmstories mapping A Towerful of Mice's "possibility space"; critics on Hearts of Stone's length (about ten hours) and range of tone. https://techraptor.net/content/tag/konrad-tomaszkiewicz ; https://vulkk.com/2016/05/07/witcher-3-qa-cdprs-quest-designers/ ; https://www.gamepro.de/artikel/the-witcher-3-quest-kein-dialog-in-40-seiten,3427812.html ; https://80.lv/articles/the-witcher-3-developer-on-hard-decisions-killing-important-characters/ ; https://www.gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2016/08/20/the-story-behind-the-story-of-the-witcher-3.aspx ; https://filmstories.co.uk/?p=83020 ; https://www.pcgamer.com/au/the-witcher-3-hearts-of-stone-review
